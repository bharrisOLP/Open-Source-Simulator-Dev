## Findings

1. **major: the tolerance is not a bound at tol 1e-9 and 1e-12. One ray gives up to 1.9 tol.**
   `optics_gpu/src/optics_gpu/pop.py:658-663` (nufft_width: "here tol bounds the largest error relative to
   sum_r |a_r|"; w = ceil(log10(1/tol)) + 2), `optics_gpu/README.md:168-171` ("Here tol bounds the largest error
   ...", "the tested errors are at most 0.92 tol (one ray, the worst case)"), `optics_gpu/README.md:289`,
   `optics_gpu/tests/test_nufft.py:118-134`.
   - Evidence (GPU computer, `image_sum(method="nufft")`, one ray with |a| = 1 on a 128 x 128 grid of pitch 2 um,
     against a long-double plane-wave reference):
     - tol 1e-12: L = M = -0.12671 gives 1.84e-12 (1.84 tol). 300 random directions with |L|, |M| < 0.38 gave a
       worst value of 1.86e-12.
     - tol 1e-9: L = M = -0.28210 gives 1.68e-9 (1.68 tol). L = -0.28210, M = 0 gives 1.38e-9.
   - Cause: for Nx = 128, n1 = 2 Nx = 256, so the edge mode is at |m| / n1 = 0.25. The module's own
     `_es_spread_1d` and `_es_transform` give these largest 1-D errors over f (8000 values in one period) and all
     modes: 1e-9: 8.4e-10 (0.84 tol), and 1e-12: 1.0e-12 (1.0 tol). The x and y errors add in 2-D, so the worst
     case is about 2 times the 1-D error. A scan over Nx 64, 100, 120 and 128 gives these 2-D worst cases: 0.73, 0.62,
     0.79, 1.68 and 2.13 tol for tol 1e-2, 1e-4, 1e-6, 1e-9 and 1e-12.
   - The test misses this case for two reasons. The 101-point grid gives n1 = 216, so the modes stop at
     |m| / n1 = 0.23. Also, 20 random directions do not reach the worst f. The same scan on the 101-point grid
     gives 1.11 tol at 1e-12.
   - The many-ray results (err_* in the bench, 0.05 tol or less) are correct, because the errors of different
     rays do not add in phase. But the README and the docstring state a bound for the worst ray.
   - Fix: use one more point, w = ceil(log10(1/tol)) + 3. With the same scan, the 2-D worst cases are then
     0.08, 0.05, 0.10, 0.16 and 0.60 tol. Then measure the bench rows and the README numbers again. As an
     alternative, state the bound as about 2.2 tol. In both cases, add a one-ray test on a 128 x 128 grid
     (n1 = 2 Nx) at the worst f on both axes (f = L h / lambda near -0.16 and -0.36 cycles per step).

2. **major: for image grids of about 2800 x 2800 points or more, the spread uses one ray per chunk.**
   `optics_gpu/src/optics_gpu/pop.py:732` (`max(1, (SUM_ELEMENTS - copies * n1 * n2 * zb - ALLOC_SLACK) //
   per_ray)`), `optics_gpu/src/optics_gpu/pop.py:725` and `optics_gpu/README.md:186` (the stated exception).
   - Evidence: with the default budget, `_nufft_steps(None, nz, n1, n1, 14)` returns a chunk of 1 ray for
     Nx 2800 (n1 5760), 2900, 3000 and 4096, for 1 plane and for 21 planes. Nx 2048 gives 24272 rays. When
     n1 n2 + ALLOC_SLACK is above SUM_ELEMENTS, the numerator is negative.
   - A reproduction with the same state on a small grid (SUM_ELEMENTS patched to 2^18, 256 x 256 grid, 3000
     rays): the default chunk took 0.754 s, and `ray_chunk=3000` took 0.004 s (190 times faster). For 5e6 rays,
     one plane would take about 20 minutes, not about 1 s.
   - The README and the docstring say only that a batch is one plane and that the peak is above the budget.
     They do not say that the chunk falls to one ray. The direct sum has a floor in the same case
     (`_planes_step` returns GROUP_RAYS).
   - Fix: give the chunk a floor (for example GROUP_RAYS or 4096 rays) when the fine grid alone fills the budget,
     and document it. Add a test of `_nufft_steps` for Nx 4096.

3. **minor: far from the axis or at a large dz, the float64 phase limits the error of both methods.** The README
   does not say this. `optics_gpu/README.md:168`.
   - Evidence: grid near (3000, -2500) um, dz 0, 5000 and -20000 um. For one ray (L 0.3, M -0.3), the non-uniform
     FFT error is 2.7e-12 and the direct-sum error is 1.2e-12, against a long-double reference. Both are above
     tol 1e-12. For 1500 random rays, the errors per sum|a| are 4.7e-13 and 4.1e-13. The non-uniform FFT is not
     worse in practice.
   - Fix: add one sentence. The error is at least about eps k |L' x_ref + M' y_ref + N' dz| per ray (the phase
     reference in float64), for both methods.

4. **minor: some README numbers come only from development runs.** No log or test supports them:
   - "one ray gave up to 5.9 tol" with the width of the paper (`optics_gpu/README.md:169`).
   - "1.3e-13 at w 15" (`optics_gpu/README.md:171`).
   - "the FFT step had a peak of 4.2 n1 n2 zb, observed".
   - "1.05 ... and 1.02" with 4 n1 n2 batches (Checks table).
   - "float32 atomic adds were much faster" (`optics_gpu/README.md:510`).

   These numbers are labelled as development runs, and they do not drive a result. Fix: log them, make them part
   of a test, or remove the numbers and keep the decision.

5. **minor: the quadrature of the kernel transform contributes about 0.1 tol at tol 1e-12.**
   `optics_gpu/src/optics_gpu/pop.py` (NUFFT_QUAD = 128). Against 2000 nodes, the relative difference of psi(m)
   is 1.1e-13 at w 14 and 7.2e-10 at w 4. The cost is small, so 256 or more nodes (or a check against more nodes)
   would remove this part of the error budget near the rounding floor.
