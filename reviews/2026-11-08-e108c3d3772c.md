1. **major**: suite/README.md:388 (also :374). The README says for 10 ns: "The shortest L_pi is 0.362 mm at 1.05 V".
   The logged 10 ns rows of the same file (suite-pin.jsonl, `tau_s` 1e-8) give L_pi = 0.2178 mm at 1.3 V (P_pi
   60.8 mW, I_pi 46.8 mA, net dneff +3.56e-3, core rise 62.1 K), and 0.549 mm at 1.25 V. So 0.362 mm is the shortest
   L_pi on the carrier side of the cross-over only; the statement is false for the logged range 0.8 to 1.3 V. Line
   374 ("the net change has its largest size at 1.0 V", 1 ns) is true only in the tabulated range to 1.2 V: above
   the cross-over the thermal net change grows with the bias (at 10 ns it is already larger at 1.3 V than at any
   carrier-side bias). Fix: say "below the cross-over (the carriers win)" for both statements, and give the 1.3 V
   row (0.218 mm, 60.8 mW, 62 K) as the thermal-side value at 10 ns.

2. **major**: suite/README.md:454-456 and :474-477 (thermal boundary conditions; the question of the lead). The
   README states the model ("no metal contacts as heat sinks", adiabatic sides), but not the direction or the size
   of its effect, and it reports the cross-over to 0.1 mV and "the place of the heat matters at the level of a few
   percent" as if the grid and the heat distribution were the main uncertainties of dneff_thermal. Reviewer runs
   (scratch script, 1 ns, 1.0 and 1.2 V, the same carriers and mode grid; base reproduces the logged 6.920e-4 and
   4.154e-3 and the core rise 3.676 and 22.05 K):
   - thermal window and substrate to +-20 um (adiabatic sides 20 um away), substrate 3 um: dneff_thermal 6.5e-4 and
     3.91e-3 (-6 %); the two-bias cross-over estimate moves +23 mV. Substrate 18 um deep, +-20 um: -4.4 %.
   - a crude heat sink (the p+/n+ slab continues to +-5 um, the sides of the window at 300 K): dneff_thermal 2.5e-4
     and 1.42e-3 (2.8 times smaller), core rise 1.34 and 7.53 K, and NO cross-over up to 1.2 V (net dneff -2.08e-3
     at 1.2 V: the carriers win).
   So the window width alone changes dneff_thermal by more than all the grid sensitivities that the README reports,
   and a heat path through the slab to the contacts can remove the cross-over. The slab in oxide with no heat sink
   inflates dneff_thermal; the README does not say so. Fix: in the results and in Limits, say that the side
   boundaries at +-5 um give about 6 % more dneff_thermal than +-20 um, that a heat path to the contacts lowers
   dneff_thermal (by up to about 3 times in a crude test) and can move the cross-over above 1.2 V, so the cross-over,
   the sign change and "above that bias the heat wins" are results of this thermal model, not of a PIN device. Give
   the cross-over to 1 mV at most. Optional: a bench option for the window width.

3. **minor**: suite/tests/test_pin.py:82 and suite/README.md:400 (the check "the sum of the heat source = I' V").
   The summation by parts holds for ANY cell potentials, not only for the quasi-Fermi potentials: the reviewer
   replaced phi_n and phi_p by random numbers (sigma 0.3 V) and got sum H / I' V - 1 = -3.3e-13 (1.0 V) and 4.6e-14
   (1.2 V), with about half of the face terms negative. So this check tests the continuity residual and the flux
   plumbing, not the local heat distribution; only the non-negativity check (test_pin.py:85) tests the local
   values. Fix: say in the checks table that the sum is an identity of the discrete scheme; optionally add a local
   check (for example the Joule term of a face against I^2 h / (q mu n L) in a quasi-neutral region of the 1-D diode).
