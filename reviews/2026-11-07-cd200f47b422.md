   symmetry but lose the x and y mirrors.

## What the reviewer tried to break

- The working tree holds the patch (`git apply --check -R` passed). The tests ran on the GPU computer
  (`bash tools/gpu_run.sh optics_gpu python -m pytest -q`, threads 4, memory fraction 0.4): 74 passed in 33.8 s,
  which agrees with the claim (9 in tests/test_sources.py: 6 parametrized + 3).
- The math of the 2 x 2 problem:
  - The Rayleigh quotient of the field a0 E0 + a1 E1 is a^H Pc a / a^H P a, with Pc[i,j] = sum conj(E_i,c) E_j,c
    and P over Ex and Ey. With P = L L^H, b = L^H a gives the Hermitian matrix Li Pc Li^H, and a = Li^H b. The
    code does this correctly (`V[:, c].conj() @ V[:, c].T`, the einsum, `Li.conj().T @ vecs[:, -1]`).
  - A phase between the two modes is free in a (complex). The tests cover it ("unitary" with phi 0.9), and the
    final phase comes from the peak of the chosen component.
  - The power is the transverse sum |Ex|^2 + |Ey|^2, not the Poynting power. This is acceptable for a
    polarization fraction and is documented.
- Realistic cross polarization (an odd-odd Ey in the x mode, a 45-degree mix): the result is the x mode to
  1.7e-17, fraction 0.99860 (that of the mode). The tests use Ey = 0 exactly, so this is new evidence that the
  cross-polarized part does not move the optimum when <EXx, EYx> = 0.
- A rotated mode that is not near-degenerate: it is taken unchanged (test). A near-diagonal one passes the tie
  rule silently (finding 4).
- Edge cases: two co-polarized degenerate modes (finding 2), duplicated and near-duplicated modes (finding 3), and
  non-orthogonal parts (finding 3).
- The Yee positions in the example: meow-gpu (operator.py, after tidy3d) places Ex at (x centers, y corners) and Ey
  at (x corners, y centers). `centroid_yee_um` uses those positions correctly. The mode file uses (xc, xc), a
  0.025 um shift along y for Ex, as documented. The overlap is scalar on |major component|, which was the method
  before this change. The phase of the source is dropped by np.abs, as before; not checked again.
- The centroid offset: it is not a Yee half-cell effect. The cause is the asymmetric core corners (finding 1).
  With a symmetric core, the centroid is (0, 0).
- The x and y sources: they are transposes. Max ||Ex(x)| - |Ey(y)|^T| is 1.3e-13 for the example eps and 2.2e-13
  for the symmetric core. This supports the claim "same efficiencies for x and y" (the lens pair, the fiber and
  the grids are symmetric under the swap).
