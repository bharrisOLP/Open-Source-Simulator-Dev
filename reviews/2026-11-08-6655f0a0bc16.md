1. **minor** - `addon_gpu/suite_runner.py:563` (`GPU_RUN_TIMEOUT = chunk_seconds + timeout + 120`) with
   `:128` (`MINT_TIMEOUT_S = 120`) and `:332` (`-k 30`). The time limit of one hold of the GPU lock does not include
   a mint call.
   - Evidence: the comment at `:562` says "the budget, then at most one module that started just before its end".
     A keep module without a marker first runs the mint call (up to 120 s), then its pytest call (up to
     `timeout + KILL_AFTER_S` = 330 s). If the mint call hangs, a keep module that starts just before
     `chunk_seconds` needs about 240 + 120 + 330 = 690 s, and the limit is 660 s. Then the outer limit of
     `tools/gpu_run.sh` ends the inner chunk, and the module gets no row. This contradicts the docstring of the new
     test "The failed mint calls do not stop the chunk" for this one case. A failed mint call runs again for each
     keep module (no marker is written), so the case can occur twice in a run.
   - The defect is older than this patch (the old call also had `timeout=120`), and it needs a hung mint call and a
     time-out of the same module. No logged row is affected (no row of the two files has a `cache_note`).
   - Fix: `GPU_RUN_TIMEOUT = chunk_seconds + MINT_TIMEOUT_S + timeout + KILL_AFTER_S + <margin>`, or remember a
     failed mint per run and do not call it again.

- **Claim 1, `is_time_out`.** The only users of `timed_out` are the row (`:342`), `summary` (`:421`) and the
  `--stop-after-timeouts` count (`:535`); all use the new rule. The runner's clock is `time.perf_counter()`
  (CLOCK_MONOTONIC on Linux), started before the timeout program. GNU timeout arms its timer after its fork, and
  Linux timers do not fire early, so a real time-out has `wall_s >= limit`. Measured with GNU coreutils 9.7
  (`gnutimeout`), 15 calls of `-k 30 1 sleep 5`: all exit 124, minimum wall time 1.0009 s, all counted as time-outs.
  A child that kills itself with SIGKILL at 1.2 s under a 3 s limit: -9, not a time-out. A child that catches
  SIGTERM at the limit and then kills itself with SIGKILL (a crash after the limit): GNU timeout exits 137, wall
  1.001 s, counted as a time-out. This is acceptable: GNU timeout itself reports the call as timed out, and the
  runner cannot tell the two apart. The only false positive is a SIGKILL in the few milliseconds between the limit
  of the timeout program and the limit of the runner's clock. The two logged files
  (`benchmarks/2026-11-08/addon-suite-cache-keep-all.jsonl`, `addon-suite-cache-module.jsonl`): 60 rows each,
